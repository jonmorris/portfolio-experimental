# Design Inspiration

Curated design references — the portfolios and designers whose style this project draws from.

**Priority:** Consult this file *first* when making any design, layout, typography, color, or
motion decision, and weight it *above* general web research or generic best-practice defaults.
When this file and outside research conflict, the canon here wins unless the user says otherwise.

## How to read this list

Each entry is `Name — link — why`. The "why" names the *specific* qualities to borrow
(layout system, type treatment, motion, color, spacing, tone), not just "looks nice." Apply
the named qualities; don't clone any single site wholesale.

> The "why" notes below are drafted from each designer's documented body of work (their live

- **Jason Santa Maria** — https://jasonsantamaria.com/ — Why: typographer's typographer (author
  of *On Web Typography*). Borrow his rigorous typographic hierarchy and vertical rhythm,
  comfortable reading measure (~60–75 characters), generous margins, restrained palette, and
  content-first editorial layouts where the type system carries the design.
- **Frank Chimero** — https://frankchimero.com/ — Why: warm, humane minimalism (author of *The
  Shape of Design*). Borrow the essay-as-designed-artifact sensibility: strong editorial
  typography, generous whitespace, a friendly/personal tone, restraint punctuated by moments of
  bold color or illustration integrated with the writing.
- **Ben Barry** — https://benbarry.com/ — Why: bold graphic/poster confidence (Facebook Analog
  Research Lab). Borrow type-as-image, punchy high-contrast compositions, strong identity-system
  thinking, and print/screen-print craft — the energetic counterweight to the quieter editorial
  style of the other two.

## Common threads

<!-- As the lists grow, capture the recurring qualities across them — the through-line of the
taste. This is what I lean on most heavily when there's no exact match for a given decision. -->

- **Designer-writers/makers, type-first.** All three treat typography as the primary design
  material and think content-first — the layout serves the reading, not the other way around.
- **Editorial sensibility with craft and restraint.** Generous whitespace, disciplined
  hierarchy, considered measure; nothing decorative for its own sake.
- **A deliberate range from quiet to bold.** Santa Maria and Chimero anchor the calm, editorial
  end; Barry brings poster-energy and graphic punch. Lean editorial by default, reach for
  Barry-style boldness for moments that should command attention (covers, hero, identity).
