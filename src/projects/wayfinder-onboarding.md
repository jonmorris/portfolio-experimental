## The problem

The funnel told a brutal story: people signed up, hit the onboarding, and
quietly disappeared. Nine steps, two dead ends, and a "set up your workspace"
screen that asked for things most users didn't have yet.

## Approach

I started in the analytics, not Figma. Mapping drop-off step by step showed the
damage was concentrated in two screens — so that's where the effort went.

- Collapsed nine steps into four, deferring everything that wasn't needed to
  reach a first "aha" moment.
- Replaced the empty-workspace screen with a templated starting point.
- Added a progress model that was honest about how much was left.

## Validating it

Five rounds of unmoderated testing in Maze caught the worst friction before a
single line of code shipped. The redesign then went out as a 50/50 A/B test.

## Outcome

- **+34%** activation (completed onboarding → first key action)
- **−41%** time to first value
- Support tickets tagged "getting started" dropped by a third

The lesson I keep coming back to: the best onboarding screen is often the one
you delete.
