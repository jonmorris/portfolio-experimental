## The problem

Three product teams, three sets of buttons, and no agreement on what "primary"
even meant. Every new feature meant re-deciding the basics, and the UI was
drifting further apart with each release.

## What I did

- **Audited the surface.** Catalogued every component, color, and spacing value
  in production. The numbers made the case for change on their own.
- **Built the token layer first.** Color, type, spacing, and radius as a single
  source of truth, themeable from day one.
- **Shipped components in tiers.** Started with the 12 components that covered
  80% of screens, then expanded based on real demand rather than a wishlist.
- **Wrote the docs as a product.** Usage guidance, do/don't examples, and
  copy-paste code — because a system nobody can use isn't a system.

## Outcome

Design-to-ship time for net-new features dropped from ~6 weeks to ~3. Just as
importantly, the teams started speaking the same language in reviews — the
arguments moved up a level, from "which blue" to "is this the right flow."

> The system isn't done — it never is. But it's now load-bearing, and that's
> the bar I was aiming for.
