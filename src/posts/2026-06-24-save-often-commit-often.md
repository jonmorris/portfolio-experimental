---
title: "Save often, commit often"
date: 2026-06-24
tags: ["post"]
summary: "I used to hit ⌘ + S constantly because I was afraid of losing work. Eventually I learned the harder lesson: the real safety net is committing often."
---

I used to hit command+S constantly. It was almost a nervous habit. Every few seconds, save, save, save. I think I believed that if I just pressed it enough, nothing bad could happen.

That worked fine until it didn't.

At some point I learned the hard way that saving a file is not the same thing as protecting your work. A saved file can still get tangled up, overwritten, or lost in all the other moving parts around it. A commit, on the other hand, gives you an actual checkpoint. It says: this is a coherent state, and I can come back to it.

That changed how I work. Now I try to commit more often, especially when I finish something meaningful or before I start taking a risk. Not because every commit has to be perfect, but because future me is usually grateful for the breadcrumb trail.

I still hit command+S sometimes. Old habits die hard. But the real reflex I want is simpler: if the work matters, commit it.
